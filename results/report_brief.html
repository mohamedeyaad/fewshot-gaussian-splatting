When do diffusion-generated views help few-shot Gaussian Splatting?

Mohamed Eyad · Robotics Engineering, Università degli Studi di Genova · 242 controlled training runs, two scenes, single 4 GB GPU

Summary. 3D Gaussian Splatting collapses when trained on few photographs: five views of truck reach 15.20 dB against 25.23 dB with all 219, a gap of 10.04 dB. Augmenting with diffusion-generated views is an obvious remedy, and this study measures it properly: three strategies × four ratios × three subset sizes × three seeds, paired within seed. The value of a synthetic view turns out to depend on how much real data already exists — outpainting is worth +0.285 dB at five real views and -0.983 dB at twenty. Four controls locate the cause in pose novelty, not in image quality or hallucinated content. That account predicts an intervention supplying geometric constraint without inventing a viewpoint should not cross over; a monocular depth prior does not, staying positive at every subset size and combining super-additively with augmentation for +0.714 dB, the largest gain measured here.

1Setup

Scene truck (Tanks & Temples, 251 images) with COLMAP poses. Every eighth image by sorted filename is held out for test and frozen across all conditions; few-shot subsets are drawn from the remaining pool by farthest-point sampling over camera centres, so the K views are well spread. The seed selects only the starting camera — three seeds share no images at k = 5, and which five you draw is worth up to 0.65 dB, so every result below is a paired difference against the same seed's own baseline. Training is 7,000 iterations at half resolution; PSNR, SSIM and LPIPS are computed on the held-out views. Repeating one configuration three times gives a noise floor of σ = 0.039 dB.

Three augmentation strategies

  • Inpainting — mask a region of a real view and regenerate it. The camera pose is copied exactly, so no new viewpoint is created.
  • Outpainting — widen the frustum and fabricate the border. Same camera centre, larger field of view: genuinely new peripheral content.
  • Pose-guided — estimate depth, warp the image to an interpolated camera, and fill the disocclusion holes with diffusion. A fully novel viewpoint.

The central finding. Diffusion augmentation helps when real views are scarce and actively harms once enough exist. The other two strategies bracket the explanation. Inpainting, which copies the camera pose exactly, is flat everywhere — between -0.213 and +0.029 dB — despite producing convincing images. Pose-guided, which invents a fully novel viewpoint, is harmful at every subset size and gets worse as k grows, reaching -1.884 dB. Ordering the three strategies by how much new camera pose they invent orders them exactly by how much damage they do.

The headline gain is PSNR-only. Outpainting at k = 5 is worth +0.285 dB but -0.0054 SSIM — small, but consistent across all three seeds. PSNR rewards the reduction in large errors where synthetic coverage fills an unobserved region; SSIM penalises the local structural noise the fabricated pixels introduce. The ordering of the three strategies by pose novelty holds on both metrics, and that ordering is this report's central claim. The sign of outpainting's benefit at k = 5 does not, and the claim is therefore conditional on the metric as well as on the training length.

Why the sign flips

A synthetic view supplies coverage and inconsistency in fixed proportion. Coverage loses value as real views accumulate — that is the per-view column of Table 1, falling tenfold. Inconsistency does not: a contradictory view is just as harmful at twenty views as at five, and arguably worse, because it now contradicts a better-determined geometry. Two terms, one decaying and one roughly constant, are sufficient to produce a sign change.

The warp-only control is the decisive one. Removing the diffusion step made pose-guided far worse, so diffusion was contributing up to 2.49 dB of repair — it is the stage holding the result up, not the stage breaking it. The damage is therefore not hallucinated content or warping artifacts but pose novelty itself. Together with the checkpoint swap (better photorealism, marginally worse result) and inpainting's flatness, three independent lines agree: what a synthetic view contributes is coverage, not photorealism.

The checkpoint swap was extended across every subset size, because a swap tested only where augmentation helps cannot tell whether the crossover belongs to Stable Diffusion 1.5. It does not: Dreamshaper-8 runs +0.193 dB at k = 5 and -0.825 at k = 20, the same reversal. Its curve also sits below SD 1.5 at every size, so it crosses earlier — at k = 10, where SD 1.5 is indistinguishable from zero (-0.003), the better-looking model is already separated and negative (-0.163). A checkpoint finetuned to make each image individually more convincing has no reason to be more consistent between images.

6Testing the mechanism

The account in §3 predicts something that can fail. If the harm comes from inconsistency rather than augmentation as such, an intervention supplying geometric constraint without inventing a viewpoint should never cross over. Monocular depth regularisation is that intervention, and it is prior work, not a contribution here: Chung et al. [2] propose the same construction for few-shot 3DGS. It is used as a control — the question is not whether a depth prior helps, which is known, but how its behaviour differs from augmentation's on identical subsets, splits and seeds. A depth network predicts an inverse-depth map per real training photo, anchored to scene scale against the sparse COLMAP points that view observes (median R² 0.964). No camera is invented, no pixel fabricated. Synthetic views receive no depth supervision — estimating depth from a fabricated image to constrain geometry would be circular.

The prediction holds. The depth prior is positive and separated from zero at every subset size, including k = 20 where outpainting costs -0.618 dB. Coverage crosses over; constraint does not. The two interventions differ in exactly one respect — whether a camera that never existed is invented — and only the one that invents a camera reverses sign.

The shape agrees as well: the prior's benefit decays with subset size (+0.259 → +0.155 dB), exactly as diminishing returns on constraint predict. It simply never turns negative, because there is no contradiction term to overtake it — the two-term account of §3 reduced to its first term and observed alone. The two also compound: at k = 5 the combination reaches +0.714 dB, the largest gain in this study, with a positive interaction at all three sizes. They repair different deficiencies — outpainting supplies peripheral content, the prior supplies constraint — which agrees with the duplication control.

The prior survives the training length that kills outpainting. At 30,000 iterations outpainting at k = 5 is worth -0.078 dB — the +0.285 measured at 7,000 is gone — while the depth prior still returns +0.208 and the combination +0.469. Fabricated pixels lose their value to longer training; a geometric constraint does not. Two caveats run against this: at k = 20 the prior is +0.130 dB at 30,000, no longer clear of the noise floor, and the super-additivity is single-scene — on drjohnson the interaction is +0.075 ± 0.672 dB, additive rather than super-additive. What holds on both scenes is only that it is never negative.

7Conclusions

  • Diffusion augmentation is not free coverage. It helps only while real views are scarce, and the crossover reproduces across an outdoor object and an indoor room.
  • The mechanism is pose novelty. Four controls exclude image quality, hallucinated hole content, warping artifacts, and mere view repetition.
  • Constraint beats coverage where data is plentiful. A depth prior never crosses over, costs +6.6% Gaussians against outpainting's +103%, and needs no diffusion model.
  • Practical recipe: at 5–10 real views use both (+0.714 and +0.294 dB); at 20, use the depth prior alone, since adding synthetic views costs about half a decibel.
  • Scale honestly: the best synthetic condition anywhere is worth 6.7× less than simply taking five more photographs.

Limitations

The 7,000-iteration operating point is load-bearing. Re-running the headline cells at 15,000 and 30,000 shows the benefit decays monotonically: outpainting at k = 5 falls from +0.285 to +0.045 to -0.078 dB, while the harm at k = 20 persists throughout (-0.618 to -0.743). The unaugmented baseline is flat from 7,000 to 15,000 (15.20 → 15.22 at k = 5, inside the noise floor) and lower by 30,000 (15.04), so the setting is not mis-chosen — 30,000 is past this regime's optimum rather than better converged, and few-shot 3DGS overfits long before it. The positive result is nonetheless conditional on early stopping. The depth prior is not: it survives 30,000.

The K views are chosen well, and augmentation does not rescue views that are not. Every result above uses farthest-point sampling. Repeating the sweep on uniformly random draws — which cover the scene worse (worst gap 5.15 against 3.54) — does not give a larger benefit, as a purely coverage-driven account would predict: +0.261 against +0.285 dB at k = 5, and more harmful at k = 20 (-0.929 against -0.618). Outpainting widens the frustum of cameras that already exist, so it cannot reach regions a badly-spread set never came near — though that reading is post-hoc. The defensible claim is the negative one.

Three seeds supports a consistency check (|mean| > σ), not a formal significance test. The crossover is bracketed between k = 5 and k = 20, not located. The depth loss weight is upstream's default, untuned for few-shot, so its gain is likely a floor. The second scene was swept for outpainting only, at one ratio; the depth prior now covers two scenes and two training lengths. Both checkpoints tested are SD 1.5-class, the only ones fitting 4 GB; multi-view-consistent generators (Zero123++, SV3D) are the natural next step and are arguably what pose-guided augmentation is really reaching for.
